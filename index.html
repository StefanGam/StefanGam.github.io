I'm a Ph.D. candidate at the Doctoral School of Cybernetics and Economic Statistics of the Bucharest University of Economic Studies (ASE), working on the thesis Risk Models for Digital Assets under the supervision of Prof. Daniel-Traian Pele and Prof. Wolfgang Karl Härdle.

I'm also a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Doctoral Candidate at the Poznań University of Economics and Business, on industrial secondment at Royalton Partners in Luxembourg, where I maintain the CRIX and VCRIX cryptocurrency indices. Within this secondment I'm currently a Visiting Researcher at the University of Chicago, Department of Statistics, hosted by Prof. Wei Biao Wu.

I explore both traditional and digital financial markets through statistics, econometrics, data science, machine learning and artificial intelligence. My published and ongoing work spans tail-risk forecasting (Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall), cryptocurrency indices, stablecoin and DeFi risk, and LLM-based tools for financial analysis. Before my Ph.D. I earned an MSc in Applied Statistics & Data Science and a BSc in Economic Informatics, both at ASE.

Research

Ongoing work in progress.

Working paper · Ren, Hu, Wu, Găman

Expected shortfall under functional dependence

Second-order expansions and risk-constrained decisions: statistical methods for estimating expected shortfall from dependent simulation output, including bias correction, confidence intervals, a rule for choosing simulation run length, and risk-constrained choice between policies. Also shows when standard Gaussian intervals break down, under long memory, heavy-tailed losses and heavy-tailed queues.

Working paper · Chaffard, Găman, Staures, Ginavar, Jheng, Pele, Härdle

Onchain monitoring of stablecoin depeg risk

An on-chain framework that monitors and forecasts depeg risk in the USDC–USDT Uniswap V3 pool, using about 38,000 hourly observations (2022–2026) on pool liquidity, lending activity and market conditions. In the 2025–26 holdout it flagged all five depeg events a median of nine hours in advance, beating GARCH and deep-learning benchmarks.

Working paper · Jheng, Tak, Găman, Micu, Costea, Pele

FINDER-LLM: Financial anomaly detection with LLMs

A multimodal, multi-agent LLM framework that combines market indicators, chart images and financial news to score next-day downside-tail risk, with a written explanation for each score. Across five equity indices and a Treasury ETF (2025–2026), it ranks tail events better on average than LSTM benchmarks (mean ROC-AUC 0.66 vs 0.55–0.57).

Working paper · Jheng, Găman, Conda, Pele, Härdle, Teng, Osterrieder

Do established cryptocurrency findings survive extended data?

An LLM-assisted structured literature review (about 38,000 Scopus records) selects four influential cryptocurrency studies, which are then re-tested with data extended to 2025. Cryptocurrencies remain a distinct asset class that improves portfolio Sharpe ratios, and established bubble-detection models still identify speculative episodes in Bitcoin.

Working paper · Lin, Găman, Wang, Pele

Do investors buy innovation? Ethereum development milestones

How markets price Ethereum protocol upgrades (2015–2024), using event studies, synthetic controls and herding tests. Upgrades that delayed the "difficulty bomb" produced significant positive abnormal returns, fee-market reform (London) reduced volatility, and the Merge triggered herding across crypto markets.

Industry work · Royalton Partners
